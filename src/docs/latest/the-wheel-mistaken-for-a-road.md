title: The Wheel Mistaken for a Road

Every serious conversation about technology now circles a single question, and the question hides a word nobody turns over. Where is technology headed. Are we being carried somewhere we did not choose. The accelerationist answers yes, gloriously - toward a threshold past which the human is surpassed and something greater takes the wheel. The doomer answers yes, catastrophically - toward a threshold past which the human is surpassed and something monstrous takes the wheel. They agree on the physics and quarrel over the weather at the destination. Both are certain we are going somewhere. The word neither of them examines is "headed."

I want to take that word away from both of them, because it is doing all the work and hiding the mechanism. Technology is not headed anywhere. "Where is it headed" is the wrong question, and the wrongness is structural, with nothing to do with humility about the future. The essential operation of modern technology is to manufacture the "toward" - to keep the point of fulfillment permanently one increment ahead of wherever we happen to be standing. The future it promises is a distance it maintains, never a place it approaches. And a civilization that has mistaken that maintained distance for a road is in a condition the Dharmic traditions diagnosed with unnerving precision, three thousand years before there was a single machine to which the diagnosis could be attached.

## The horizon that keeps its distance

Consider how the promise actually behaves over time, because the behavior is the tell. Full self-driving was a year away in 2016, and a year away in 2019, and a year away in 2022, and the men who said so were not lying in any ordinary sense. They believed it each time, and each time the year arrived and the destination had moved on by a year. Artificial general intelligence has been a few years off for as long as there have been people paid to estimate it. Kurzweil fixed the singularity at 2045 and the number took on the fixity of scripture. This season the estimates cluster nearer: one laboratory chief expects the thing around 2027, another holds to even odds by the decade's end, and the loudest voices now name 2026 itself, the way earlier voices confidently named every year that has since gone by.

Watch the structure rather than the dates. The horizon moves at exactly the speed of the traveler. This is the defining property of a horizon and the reason the word is the right one. You may walk toward it for a lifetime and it will keep its distance to the inch, and at no point will you have gained on it, and at no point will you stop believing you are about to. The next model will be the one. The next release closes the gap. The version number climbs and the gap it was meant to close is still there, one version further on, and the certainty that it will close is not weakened by having been wrong every prior time. It is somehow strengthened. This is not how a person relates to a road. It is how a person relates to a thirst.

## Trishna, the thirst that drinking renews

The Buddha built an entire diagnosis of the human condition on this exact structure, and he named its engine with a word English happens to share at the root. Trishna, from √tṛṣ, to thirst - the same ancient root that surfaces in our own "thirst," an Indo-European inheritance carrying the same dry-throated fact into two grammars. Trishna is craving of a specific shape: the perpetual leaning-forward of the mind into a next moment that will finally satisfy. The second of the four noble truths locates the origin of dukkha, of suffering, precisely here. Not in the world's failure to hand us what we want. In the architecture of wanting itself, which fabricates a next object the instant the present one is grasped, so that satisfaction is always one acquisition away and never here.

The subtlety the tradition saw, and that our discourse has missed entirely, is that trishna is not cured by getting the thing. Getting the thing is how trishna reproduces. The wanting does not point at the object; it points at the wanting, and it uses each object as an occasion to renew itself. The man who believes the next promotion, the next house, the next arrival will still the thirst has misread the thirst as being about promotions and houses, when it is about the leaning-forward and would survive the granting of every wish he has. This is dukkha, and dukkha does not mean sorrow in the soft modern sense. From duh and kha - a bad axle-hole, a wheel that does not sit true on its hub, so that every rotation grinds. The suffering is the friction of a thing that turns wrongly. It is built into the turning.

##### Modern technological civilization is trishna given a supply chain - the leaning-forward of the mind fitted with fabrication plants and capital markets and a release schedule, so the next object is always in production and the arrival always one quarter out.

We have taken the most private and portable affliction of the human interior, the thirst that mistakes its own restlessness for the lack of a right object, and we have externalized it, industrialized it, and poured concrete foundations beneath it. The engine of wanting now has a whole civilization for a body. Every product cycle is a renewed vow that this time the object will satisfy. Every launch is the granting of a wish that, by the oldest psychology we possess, cannot still the wanting, because the wanting was never about the wish. And the wanting, once handed infrastructure, does at civilizational scale exactly what it does in the single mind. It accelerates. It has to. A thirst that could be satisfied would end; a thirst that constitutes a civilization must keep its object forever one step out, or the civilization organized around the reaching would have nothing left to do.

## Samsara, the motion mistaken for a journey

The tradition had a word for the whole condition, for the total system of thirst and its renewal, and the word is one the modern world has borrowed without grasping what it took. Samsara. From sam and √sṛ, to flow together, to run on and on, to wander continuously. Samsara is a kind of motion - perpetual, driven, recurrent - the running-on of a consciousness pushed from behind by trishna and mistaking its circular momentum for a journey toward somewhere. Not a place one is sent to. Not a sentence one serves. A momentum one is caught inside. The image the tradition gave it is exact and worth recovering. The bhavachakra, the wheel of becoming, turned ceaselessly by craving at its hub, every spoke a realm, every rotation a life, the whole apparatus spinning with tremendous energy and arriving nowhere, because a wheel does not go anywhere. It turns. The turning is the point and the trap.

Here is the thing to see, and once seen it does not close again. What we are calling acceleration is the spinning of this wheel, and we have mistaken the speed of rotation for the speed of travel. A faster wheel is still a wheel. Rotation is not translation. You may drive the bhavachakra to any velocity you please, spin it until the spokes blur to a solid disc and the individual lives run too fast to tell apart, and you will not have moved one inch toward a destination, because rotation has none. It has only more rotation, bought with more energy. The accelerationist stands beside a wheel spun to a scream and reports, truthfully, that he has never felt such speed. He takes the scream for arrival. He is living inside the oldest error the traditions ever named, experiencing samsara at a civilizational RPM and calling the blur "the future."

##### The singularity is the modern name for the point on the wheel that is always just ahead - the horizon rebranded as a destination, the receding distance sold as an approaching event.

This reframes the whole accelerationist eschatology. The singularity is the structural horizon that keeps the curve moving, the always-just-ahead that must stay just ahead, because an acceleration is a reaching, and a reaching that arrived would stop being a reaching. It is no more a coming event than the seam where sky meets water is a place one could stand. The believers have taken the receding point that keeps their wheel turning and dressed it as the destination the wheel turns toward. They have looked directly at the mechanism guaranteeing they never arrive and named it the thing they are about to arrive at. This will not be tested and found true or false in 2027 or 2045. It is a horizon, and horizons do not arrive; they recede at the speed of the one who chases them, which is why the date has moved every time the date has come, and will move again, and the moving will again be read as proof that the arrival is near.

## The borrowed eschatology

One ought to ask where the certainty of a destination came from, because it came neither from the machines nor from the data. It is older than both, and it is inherited. The picture of history as an arrow - time as a line running from a beginning toward a consummation, a real future lying ahead toward which events travel and in which they are fulfilled - is a specific civilizational bequest, and its origin is theological. It is the shape of Christian eschatology: the fall behind us, the Kingdom ahead, the Judgment at the end of the line where history is gathered up and redeemed. The modern West performed a famous operation on this inheritance. It kept the arrow and discarded the God, kept the End and discarded the Judgment, kept the promised consummation ahead and secularized it into Progress. The future became a real place, ahead, better, toward which we move, the Kingdom with the theology filed off.

The Dharmic traditions never made this move, because they never held the premise. Time, kala, is no arrow here. It turns - through the yugas, through the vast breathing of the manvantaras, through cycles nested within cycles that arrive at no terminal consummation, because the shape of time is the wheel and not the line. There is no place ahead toward which the cosmos is travelling. There is only the turning, and at every point on it the same question presses, the only one the frame recognizes: are we aligned, here, now, with rta, the deep grain of things, or grinding against it. A civilization that thinks in cycles cannot generate an accelerationism, because there is no finish line to accelerate toward and no doctrine that going faster brings the good nearer. The good is a matter of alignment - here, now, available at any point on the wheel or at none - and never a location ahead.

So when the accelerationist promises the singularity and the doomer prophesies the end, we should recognize what we are hearing. It is the Rapture, twice, in a lab coat. The eschaton immanentized in silicon, the Second Coming rescheduled to fit the compute budget, the same arrow-shaped hope and the same arrow-shaped dread the tradition secularized two centuries ago, now bolted to a technology and presented as the sober extrapolation of a curve. The accelerationists are the last and most literal believers in the Christian shape of time, having kept the eschatology so purely that they dispensed even with the God who was supposed to run it, leaving the arrow to fly at nothing, toward a consummation that is a horizon wearing the mask of an event.

## Who leads whom

Now the question we began with can be answered, and the answer stands near the older arguments about vacated seats and severed curbs without being theirs. The accelerationist and the doomer both believe there is a destination, one bright, one dark, and both believe the machine is carrying us toward it. There is no destination. There is a mechanism whose entire function is to produce the sensation of a destination, to generate the "toward" fresh each morning and keep it stocked and just out of reach. The machine leads us nowhere, expensively, at rising speed, and sells us the nowhere as a somewhere one release away. It is a device for producing, at industrial scale, the feeling of going-toward. Call it a vehicle with a bad driver and you have already conceded too much, because there is no destination a good driver could reach either.

This is the fractal the tradition always insisted upon, and it holds here without a seam. What is true of the jiva is true of the samuha. The single mind driven by trishna runs on through its lives mistaking momentum for progress, certain the next birth or the next acquisition holds the arrival, and the entire apparatus of its suffering is the machinery that keeps the arrival one step out. A civilization is a jiva at scale. Ours is a mind of that kind given a body of foundries and fiber, running on through its product cycles mistaking acceleration for progress, certain the next model holds the arrival, its whole dukkha the friction of a wheel it has spun to a scream and cannot imagine leaving, because it has forgotten - having burned the traditions that remembered - that there was ever anything to do with a wheel except turn it faster.

The tradition's answer to "where is this headed" was never a better position on the wheel. Not a faster technology, not a post-scarcity realm one turn on, not an aligned intelligence that finally steers the spin toward the good. The answer was moksha, and moksha is release from the wheel, never an advanced position on it. Nirodha, the stilling of the thirst that generates the toward, is the third of the noble truths, and it is the only exit the tradition ever claimed was real, because it is the only move that addresses the hub instead of the rim. Everything the accelerationist offers is a faster rim. The whole of what he calls the future is more circumference at higher speed. He has never once touched the hub, and the hub is thirst, and thirst will still be thirsting when the wheel has been spun past the singularity and past whatever is said to lie past that, one horizon further on, keeping its distance to the inch.

## The hub, not the rim

I do not think the answer is to stop the wheel, which cannot be done and would be its own grinding against the grain, a refusal of the human vocation to make. The work of an age that wanted to be sane would be to stop mistaking the wheel for a road - to recover the plain knowledge that rotation is not travel, that a manufactured horizon is not a destination, that a thirst institutionalized is a thirst still and will not be slaked by the object it has been built to keep just out of reach. The tradition looked at a wheel turned by craving and did not ask how to spin it faster. It asked how a man gets off. We have built the fastest such wheel in the history of the species, wired the whole earth into its rim, and taught ourselves to read the blur of it as motion toward a promised land - while somewhere in the acceleration a man with a dry throat keeps being handed the next glass and keeps being told, with each year's fresh sincerity, that this one is the one that quenches, that the arrival is close now, a year out, five at the most, the way it has been a year out for a lifetime, the way the horizon is always a year out, receding at precisely the speed of our thirst for it.
